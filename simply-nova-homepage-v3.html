Hero follows the clock. Before 11 the headline is the egg sandwich, because that's what someone reaching for their phone at 8:40 is deciding about. After 11 it's the fish. One line of copy, swapped on a schedule — no personalization engine required.

Delivery is a parameter, not a fourth door. The doors answer "which business am I transacting with." Pickup vs. delivery answers "how does it reach me" — smaller question, so it gets a control inside the door rather than a card of its own. The ZIP check fires before anyone builds a cart. Discovering at checkout that you're two blocks outside the zone is how you lose a customer and get a one-star review in the same minute.
Three visitor states, not two. In-zone, in-city-but-outside-zone, and out-of-town all want different things. The middle group is the one that gets hurt if you conflate "local" with "deliverable" — they should land on pickup, not on a delivery form that rejects them. Try the Visitor toggle above.
Catering is a real cart. Headcount is the input, not tray quantity — nobody thinks in platters, they think in guests. The 48-hour cutoff is enforced at add-to-cart, not at checkout. The thing this needs that the other two doors don't: a per-day capacity ceiling, or the cart will cheerfully accept forty platters for Sunday.
