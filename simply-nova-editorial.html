      Break-fast for twelve, without the arithmetic

      Yom Kippur ends at sundown and everyone arrives hungry at once. How much to order, what to put out first, and what you can buy the day before.

      Written by
      Felix PlacenciaCo-owner
      Published
      September 11, 2026
      Reading time
      6 minutes
      Filed under
      Seasonal

      The break-fast is the easiest meal of the year to get wrong. Nobody has eaten since the night before, every guest arrives in the same ten minutes, and whoever is hosting has been fasting too. The job isn’t cooking. It’s having everything on the table before the first coat comes off.

      That’s why the break-fast became an appetizing table. It’s all cold, it’s all ready, and none of it needs a stove: bagels, a schmear, a platter of fish, something sweet. The only hard part is how much.

      Start with the fish

      Sean, at the slicerPick up sliced fish the morning of, not the day before. It keeps, but the edges dry out and it stops folding.

      Plan on about two ounces of sliced fish a head if there’s plenty else on the table. For twelve people that’s a pound and a half, and it shouldn’t all be the same fish. Gaspé nova is the one everybody eats. Add half a pound of something with an opinion, like belly lox or sable, for the people who have been thinking about it since noon.

      Lay the fish out last, straight from the fridge onto the platter, twenty minutes before sundown. Cold-smoked salmon tastes of almost nothing when it’s cold; by the time the shofar sounds it will be exactly right.

      Nobody has eaten since the night before, and every guest arrives in the same ten minutes.

      Then everything that holds

      Sean, at the registerEveryone says they want plain. The scallion runs out first. Get one of each for every six people.

      Whitefish salad and cream cheese can be bought the day before and nobody will know. Bagels too, if you have them sliced before they go in the bag: a day-old bagel sliced cold toasts better than a fresh one you’re hacking at with a bread knife.

      Finish with something that doesn’t need a plate. A chocolate babka, sliced thick, disappears faster than you’d think from people who have just eaten a pound and a half of fish.

      Or skip the arithmetic entirely. The whole table is below, scaled to however many are coming.
